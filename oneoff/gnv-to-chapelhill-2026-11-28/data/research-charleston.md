# Research: Charleston SC (+ Mount Pleasant, North Charleston)

Position: **Sat 5:00 / arrive 15:50 / Sun 4:41 (292 mi) / detour +1:22 / frac 0.52.**

## A correction to the research's headline

The agent concluded Charleston is "**not really a detour**." **That framing is wrong, and
its own numbers say so.** It cited direct 513.5 mi (rome2rio) against 304 + 308 = 612 mi via
Charleston — **+98 miles** — then characterised that as "barely a detour."

My Valhalla measurement: the Charleston corridor is **613.8 mi / 10.22 h** against a
530.8 mi / 8.31 h baseline, i.e. **+83 miles and +1:22**. Routing Gainesville → Charleston →
Chapel Hill as a three-point request gives **+1:22** over the unconstrained fastest route.
The two mileage figures agree within 15 miles. So the measurement is not in dispute — only
the adjective is.

**Charleston costs roughly an extra hour and twenty minutes of driving, which is nearly
double the stated 45-minute budget.** What the agent got right is the *split*: at frac 0.52
Charleston is the most evenly balanced stop on the route, which is exactly the position the
brief asked for. Both things are true — it is well-placed and over budget.

## The one outstanding attraction

**Holiday Festival of Lights**, James Island County Park (871 Riverland Dr) — **confirmed
open every night Nov 13–Dec 31, 17:30–22:00**, and Nov 28 is officially priced a
**"yellow" (moderate)** night on the ticket calendar.
- **$25/vehicle online, $35 at the gate.** Buy online.
- A **3-mile drive-through** route, 750+ displays, 2M lights, **unlimited loops on one
  entry** — plus a walk-around village with a train ($5/person), carousel ($2), climbing
  wall ($4), **free marshmallow fire pits**, a 50-ton sand sculpture and Santa nightly.
- **This is the best structural fit found anywhere on the corridor for this family:** the
  best part happens with the kids strapped in and warm, after five hours in the car.
- Enter **from Folly Road via Griffith Blvd or Grimball Rd** — not the Central Park Rd
  route GPS defaults to. Arrive right at 17:30 to beat the 18:30–20:00 peak.
- <https://www.ccprc.com/holiday-festival-of-lights/> · <https://www.ccprc-tickets.com/>

**Alternative:** **Aquarium Aglow** at the SC Aquarium — the full aquarium after dark with
light installations and **Scuba Claus diving the Great Ocean Tank nightly**. Nov 28 is an
event night; timed entry 17:30–19:30, open to 21:00. Live prices pulled for Nov 28:
**$125.80** for four at the 17:30–18:00 slots, **$109.80** at 18:30 or later. Four times the
cost of the lights for a less car-friendly experience. <https://scaquarium.org/aquarium-aglow>

**Also on Nov 28:** SC Stingrays vs Savannah Ghost Pirates (ECHL hockey), North Charleston
Coliseum, **18:05** — loud, fast, cheap, strong with boys this age. Single-game prices not
published. **Mount Pleasant Pier** (1,250 ft, longest pedestrian pier in the Southeast,
lit Ravenel Bridge overhead) is free and open to 22:00.

## What the arrival time kills

Everything marquee. At a 15:50 arrival with sunset 17:14:

- **Patriots Point / USS Yorktown** — the walk-through aircraft carrier, 27 aircraft, the
  destroyer *Laffey*. **Museum 10:00–17:00 but the ticket office closes at 16:00**, and it
  needs 3–4 hours. Open Nov 28, but unreachable on arrival. **$102** for four + parking
  $5.50/hr ($25/day max). *This is a Sunday-morning activity or nothing.*
- **Children's Museum of the Lowcountry** — closes 17:00, **$52**, and it targets "birth to
  age 10," so a 9-year-old is at the top edge. No Nov 26–29 hours notice posted.
- **Charles Towne Landing** — visitor centre 16:00, Animal Forest 16:00. Worse, the replica
  ship *Adventure* **departs each winter for maintenance** (mid-November in the comparable
  year) — assume no ship.
- **Fort Sumter ferry** — last departure ~14:30–14:45, round trip 2h15. Impossible. **$138.**
- **Angel Oak** — parking gate closes 17:00, 30+ min out, and a 400-year-old tree in the
  dark is nothing.
- **Magnolia Plantation** — 09:00–16:00, last ticket an hour before close.
- **The Battery / White Point Garden** — free, but "9:00 to sunset." The Civil War cannons
  are genuinely great for these ages, and reachable only if arriving before ~16:45.

## Lodging — a structural finding

**Every indoor pool in the Charleston metro is in North Charleston or Summerville.** Hilton's
own indoor-pool filters return **zero** properties for Charleston proper and **zero** for
Mount Pleasant.

- **Recommended: Embassy Suites Charleston Airport & Convention Center** (5055 International
  Blvd, I-526 Exit 16) — **indoor heated pool** per four independent sources including
  Hilton's own filter (one aggregator dissents — call to confirm), free cooked-to-order
  breakfast, **two-room suites with a sofa bed that genuinely sleep 4**, parking **$10**.
  From-rates seen $118–225, **none for Nov 28**.
- Backups, all indoor heated pool + free breakfast + free parking: **Hyatt Place Charleston
  Airport** (from $119), **SpringHill Suites North Charleston** (7535 North Forest Dr — *not*
  SpringHill Charleston Airport on W Montague, which is outdoor), **Staybridge Suites North
  Charleston** (full kitchens, from $152), **Home2 Suites Summerville** (from $136, and
  25 mi further up I-26, shaving Sunday).
- **Confirmed outdoor-only — the family-chain trap:** Residence Inn, Homewood Suites,
  Hampton Inn & Suites and Home2 Suites at Charleston Airport; North Charleston Marriott,
  Aloft, Element, Courtyard, Fairfield, TownePlace, Crowne Plaza. Several are **seasonally
  closed** entirely: Hilton Garden Inn Airport, DoubleTree North Charleston, Wingate.
- Two all-weather exceptions: **Charleston Harbor Resort** (Mount Pleasant) has a "year round
  heated cabana pool" and is **walking distance to the Yorktown**; **Charleston Place** has a
  rooftop saltwater pool with a **retractable glass roof** — but $400–600+ plus a $40 nightly
  amenity fee plus $62 valet.

**Downtown parking: my $25–40 estimate was too low.** Verified on hotels' own pages:
Charleston Place **$62** valet; The Dewberry **$58** with "self-parking is not offered";
Francis Marion **$40 + tax**. The real band is **$34–62**. City garages cap at $24/day, with
$5-after-17:00 flats at Majestic, East Bay/Prioleau and 93 Queen.

**Zones:** downtown $180–900+ with mostly no self-park at all; Mount Pleasant ~$180 and
**no indoor pool anywhere**, plus the worst Sunday departure (bridge + peninsula to reach
I-26); North Charleston ~$90–160, free parking, every indoor pool, and it sits **at the
I-26/I-526 junction — straight onto I-26 W toward I-95**.

**No Nov 28 2026 rate was obtainable anywhere** — Hilton, Hyatt, Marriott, IHG and Choice
all 403, Expedia 429, Booking empty. **No occupancy data, ADR series, or evidence of surge
pricing or minimum stays was found either way.** The one hard demand signal is indirect: the
light festival prices by expected crowd level and Nov 28 is a tier above its cheapest nights.
Charleston's actual November hotel crunch is **Nov 13–15** (Food & Wine Classic, YALLFest),
not Thanksgiving weekend.

## Verdict

**The best drive-through light show on the route, at the best-balanced position, for +1:22
of driving the owner said they did not want to spend.** It becomes clearly worth it only if
Sunday morning is spent on the USS Yorktown (10:00–12:30, $102) — which converts the detour
from "nice lights" into the thing the boys remember, at the cost of arriving in Chapel Hill
around 18:00 on the worst traffic evening of the year. As a pure overnight waypoint, the
+1:22 is hard to justify against Columbia's confirmed zoo lights at +0:49 with no I-95.
