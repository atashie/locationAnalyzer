# Pass 25 — fitness for purpose

**The effort is currently disproportionate and elaborating the wrong axis.** The architecture has useful safeguards, but S3 is becoming a comparator certification project while the decisions it exists to support remain unmeasured. For a friends-and-family location finder, a reproducible approximation checked against actual destinations, journeys and browser output would improve trust more than another round of universal arithmetic and provenance rules. The large historical verdict flips justify retaining targeted correctness work; they do not justify withholding every development experiment until a portable reference is frozen. Keep the separation of claims, narrow the first release, and spend the next week obtaining evidence that could change the grid, encoding or release decision.

**Judgements on the six hypotheses**

**1. Proportionality: agree, with an important qualification.** The owner's tolerance is median absolute boundary error ≤10% of the limit and p90 ≤20% ([decisions, row 12](../plans/2026-11-03-v2-rethink-decisions.md)). That is 60/120 seconds at ten minutes. It is neither a promise of identical binary64 outputs nor permission to introduce arbitrary 59-second pipeline errors: routing, snapping, quantization and reality share the total budget.

The distinction is between small changes to an answer and small changes to a selection rule:

| Work being settled | Can it change a whole-minute product verdict? | Fitness implication |
|---|---|---|
| A 1e-9 s answer tolerance; observed 1.42e-14 s representation spread | The cited tiny spread is not a material duration error. Such differences change an encoded minute only if they straddle a rounding boundary; an unrounded comparator can also straddle an exact threshold. Neither makes the underlying journey materially different. | Record near-boundary ambiguity; do not make universal representation equivalence a prerequisite to a development run. |
| A 0.005 m connector difference | Direct walking time is about 0.004 s. But retaining a different attachment can select a different network journey. | Check the resulting journey/verdict, not the metre count alone. |
| Exact residual equality, occurrence identity, cost summation/grouping | Yes: candidate membership or cost argmin can change discontinuously, selecting hundreds of seconds of different travel time. | Keep occurrence, direction, attachment and selection regression fixtures. “Below 60 seconds in the input” is no defence. |
| Exact identity of tied reconstruction/provenance | Sometimes: identity can affect selection or a reported boundary-touch diagnostic. Otherwise it need not change the map's time or verdict. | Require identity sufficient to explain/replay the chosen answer; defer stronger uniqueness unless a released feature consumes it. |

The 1,000-versus-1-second arithmetic example and 833-versus-167-second merged-occurrence example are consequential ([design §§2.4, 13](../plans/2026-11-04-s3-measurement-design.md)); they survive whole-minute encoding. Conversely, 600.035 versus 599.979 seconds flips an exact ten-minute comparator verdict but both round to ten minutes. A comparator verdict flip is therefore not automatically an encoded-map flip. Whole-minute rounding itself can include 629 seconds at a ten-minute limit: that is a real, bounded 29-second overrun to measure.

The distinction has been drawn locally: the [build record](../experiments/s3-instrument-build.md) separates time tolerance from cost argmin and provenance, and pass 23 separates actual predicates from conservative tolerance neighbourhoods. It has **not** become a product-error budget, a work-priority rule or a stopping rule. The counterexamples establish possibility, not their prevalence on Triangle data. Sampling is how to learn the latter.

**2. Measurement aimed at the wrong claim: substantially agree.** E1 is necessary: a raster/export pipeline must not corrupt an engine's answer. It also includes the category selection gap by comparing against objective 1, so it is stronger than merely checking that the pipeline repeats its cost-selected output. But shared missing roads, wrong destinations, inaccessible entrances and walking-speed assumptions can make both sides agree and both wrong.

E3 is honestly labelled, but inadequate to the stated tolerance. Its 5–8 convenience journeys cannot establish a population p90; its signed median can cancel early and late errors, and its 25% bias / gross-overrun rules do not test median absolute error ≤10% and p90 ≤20% ([design §3](../plans/2026-11-04-s3-measurement-design.md), [`e3.py`](../../v2/locfinder/measure/e3.py)). Keep FAILED/UNVERIFIED. Add empirical absolute errors, threshold overruns and usable-destination checks, then actually collect them. More scorer tests cannot substitute for an untaken walk.

**3. Scope versus product risk: partly agree.** The upper-bound selection issue is not absent: E1 explicitly includes it, and the [layer contract §9](../plans/layer-contract.md) already reports a maximum 668.6-second bracket gap and 205 threshold disagreements at ten minutes. Those are neither a real-world error rate nor proof that every time-minimising route is usable. The owner's H2 memory correctly forbids using the cost-selected surface as a time-minimising counterfactual baseline; the same principle is documented in the contract.

`NO_PATH` being unreachable is a reason to preserve honest UNKNOWN/bounded answers, not to force a production example of every synthetic status. The 95%+ POI-runtime figure in [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) is V1 performance evidence, not evidence of POI inaccuracy or a V2 timing estimate. It does make data acquisition/cache behaviour worth measuring before polishing nanoseconds.

The scope mismatch is release scope: the [execution plan §1](../plans/2026-11-04-s3-execution-plan.md) explicitly covers only walk/grocery/Triangle, excludes field journeys, and records other modes/Valhalla on the old grid. That is sufficient for a clearly restricted first release. It cannot support a general location-finder accuracy claim. Aligning the independent reference is real work; silently comparing the existing rasters is not a shortcut.

**4. Opportunity cost: agree; a stopping rule should already have fired.** There is a statistical stopping/replacement policy, but no engineering rule that ends comparator elaboration. Pass 21 supplied a finish line; passes 22–23 showed that the proposed guard could itself reject correct implementations and miss undeclared dimensions ([execution-plan introduction](../plans/2026-11-04-s3-execution-plan.md)). That is evidence against the universal-freeze ambition as the next milestone. It is not evidence that a useful experimental comparator cannot be written.

**5. Legibility: partly agree.** Exact one-sided bounds are cheap now that T1 exists; retain them when making rate claims. Nine-cell accounting is compact and prevents disappearing failures. Input identities and retained attempts prevent misleading reruns. The excess is requiring the whole apparatus to be completed and understood before making a reversible development choice. Put reproducibility in an appendix/bundle and the release decision on one page. Do not call an exploratory result certified.

**6. Missing user-facing measurements: agree.** The most important missing quantity is **bad recommendations among the results a user sees**. E1b is deliberately `Pr(product PASS and comparator FAIL)` over all structures/area, not `Pr(actual failure | displayed PASS)` ([design §3](../plans/2026-11-04-s3-measurement-design.md)). If only 10% of locations pass, a 1% full-frame error rate can coexist with 10% erroneous inclusions among passing locations. This is illustrative arithmetic, not an observed rate. Keep the existing estimand; add a separately named conditional reliability measure with its own denominator and uncertainty. Apply it to actual displayed results, including combined criteria, rather than presenting the full-frame bound as the chance a recommendation is wrong.

Also missing from S3 is a measured answer to “does a usable grocery actually exist at the promised endpoint?” A closed store can pass E1 and E2 because the destination list is shared. The plan acknowledges category precision/recall and entrance usability in S6.3, but deferring **grocery** validity until breadth expansion leaves the first category's promise unchecked. Geocoding is similarly upstream: the plan records wrong-entity and missing-entity examples in S2.1; routing precision cannot rescue the wrong hospital. The task is to verify present user behaviour, not assume every historical example remains broken.

**Ranked next actions**

Costs are rough focused developer effort plus human participation, not measured estimates. These recommendations amend sequencing/claims; they do not silently change the owner's existing thresholds or authorize implementation in this read-only pass.

1. **Run a small audit of actual recommendations now.** About 1 developer day plus 3–6 household person-hours initially. Start with the planned 5–8 journeys; recruit family for roughly 20–30 distinct origins as feasible, concentrating on shaded homes near 10/15-minute boundaries and on plausible barriers. Preserve targeted and randomly selected cases as separate groups. Record the exact map result, chosen destination/entrance, date, observed door-to-usable-entrance time and access problems; inspect excluded controls too. Report signed and absolute errors and empirical tails without claiming a population p90 from this small convenience sample. A grocery that is closed or unusable is a failed recommendation, not a row to discard. This directly retires gross “ten minutes means twenty” and nonexistent-destination risks, while exposing which modelling assumptions deserve work. Keep the formal accuracy claim UNVERIFIED.

2. **Build T3 as a versioned development comparator and take the first matched measurements.** Time-box to 2–3 developer days, including a small pilot. Keep independent toy expectations and historical large-flip fixtures; validate inputs, preserve distinct occurrences/directions and both connectors, and use separate time/cost objective views. Pin one implementation/runtime and save its version and actual operands. Declare a realistic supported domain; refuse invalid inputs and retain unresolved cases. Do not claim cross-implementation arithmetic/provenance equivalence. Compare 100/200 m at the same residential and ground coordinates; report paired seconds, encoded verdicts and category-selection gaps. Benchmark before estimating the full run. This retires the risk of choosing a grid by argument. If the time box expires, reduce the comparator's domain or the experiment's scope and report that limit; do not launch another open-ended universal-conformance pass. Known wrong answers remain blockers for affected cases, not a licence to count them as agreements.

3. **Finish and exercise the published browser path before freezing bytes.** About 1–2 developer days, plus access to a representative family phone. Keep whole minutes as the current owner-selected default; benchmark half-minute/integer-second alternatives only as explicit development comparisons, including compressed size and phone decode/render latency. Run real browser fixtures through writer, export, decoding, lookup and card combination. Check rounded threshold edges, UNKNOWN versus FAIL, horizon equality, legacy 251–254 dispatch, mismatched grids and outside-footprint queries. The 120-minute decision does not itself fix legacy decoding; [`grid.py`](../../v2/locfinder/grid.py) still permits 0–254 minutes and the [spike worker](../../v2/spikes/g3-render/worker.js) recognises one nodata code. Versioned migration and browser validation are already required by S3.5/T7: keep them. This retires errors in the answer actually shown, which Python comparator fidelity cannot detect.

4. **Audit grocery truth, resolution and coverage as first-release requirements.** About 1–2 developer days plus 2–4 hours of local checking, overlapping action 1. Inspect a small mixed set of supplied groceries and known local groceries missing from the list; verify category, public access, usable entrances and freshness. Use cheap owner corrections/source updates rather than a new paid data dependency. Exercise familiar place names with users, showing candidate identity and map pin for confirmation. Measure failed/ambiguous/wrong accepted resolutions. Test outside-Triangle requests with no coverage presented explicitly; do not extrapolate or show absence as an exclusion. S3 already specifies outside-footprint UNKNOWN, so the gap is end-to-end enforcement and comprehension. This retires shared-source and wrong-place failures invisible to engine agreement. Set a practical release coverage/handling requirement before calling the product adequate; E1 can pass an all-abstaining product today.

5. **Publish a short decision report, then run a bounded household release.** About half a developer day, followed by 3–5 short sessions. State walk/grocery/Triangle scope, measured coverage, observed overruns, conditional reliability, unresolved cases, chosen grid/format and measured phone behaviour. Mark untested modes/categories unavailable. Ask households to complete a real search and explain what shading and unavailable areas mean; retain combined-criteria checks when that feature is offered. Keep the detailed cross-tab, existing interval calculations, raw rows and version/source identities behind the report. Log problems by user harm and measured frequency. This retires misunderstanding and overgeneralisation; it also tests whether the product helps anyone make the intended decision.

**Cut/defer:** further general-purpose mutation/invariant expansion, portable bitwise comparator equivalence, unique reconstruction beyond consumed outputs, and certification of every future mode before this narrow release. Preserve existing useful tests and historical attempts; stop producing more apparatus without a named product risk or experiment it enables. A semantic correction versions the comparator and invalidates affected results. For any later formal certification, predeclare the final run and retain all attempts; a development pilot must never be relabelled as that run.

**A sufficient release report:** one page answering “what can I search, where, how often is an answer unavailable, what happened on the journeys we checked, what errors remain, and why this grid/encoding?” Include counts and denominators, actual browser/phone measurements and the explicit UNVERIFIED real-world claim. Keep the reproducibility bundle as supporting evidence. Freeze after these measurements justify a stable choice, not as the price of learning what choice to make.

**Do not weaken:** matched coordinates; independent residential sampling beyond the predicted boundary; paired numeric values; retained absences and unresolved observations; objective-1/category-gap accounting; versioned status semantics; actual browser checks; and the prohibition on turning engine agreement or eight successful walks into proof of real-world accuracy. Those safeguards serve this product. The universal freeze currently standing in front of its first experiment does not.
