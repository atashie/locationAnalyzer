# Phase 1 Plan: the first complete slice

> **For Claude:** REQUIRED SUB-SKILL: Use superpowers:executing-plans to implement this plan
> task-by-task.

**Goal:** one metro (the widened Triangle) in which a household can state real constraints — arrive
by 08:45 at *each* of two workplaces, walk to groceries, drive to an airport — see the region that
satisfies all of them simultaneously, understand why anywhere is excluded, and hand off to a real
listings search.

**Architecture:** precompute arrival-time surfaces per (mode, category) — walking from our own
graph, driving and cycling from Valhalla `/expansion`, transit from MOTIS `one-to-all` — all through
one snapping, status and encoding contract; publish immutable bundles with a manifest switched
atomically; evaluate the constraint algebra over the encoded surface in the browser or a Worker;
serve arbitrary anchors on demand from the single VPS through the *same* encoding path.

**Tech stack:** Python 3.13 / uv, Valhalla 3.9.1, MOTIS, DuckDB, FastAPI, MapLibre GL, Cloudflare
Pages + R2.

**Date:** 2026-11-03 · **Branch:** `v2-rethink` · **Revision 2**, restructured after Codex
adversarial pass 7 (`../research/14-codex-adversarial-review-pass7-phase1-plan.md`) found 5 critical
and 11 high defects in revision 1, including a reproduced correctness bug in the expansion adapter
and a circular task ordering. **Status:** for owner review.

**Inputs:** `../experiments/phase0-decisions.md` (revision 4), `../research/08-synthesis.md` §7,
Codex passes 5, 6 and 7.

## 0. What changed from revision 1, and why

Revision 1 put an encoding freeze first and a boundary audit second. Both were wrong, and the
reasons are worth keeping because they are the same reasons the Phase 0 write-ups needed six
correction passes.

**The ordering was circular.** Revision 1's A1 froze a sentinel scheme and a numeric resolution,
but *what statuses exist* is decided by the constraint algebra (how an unknown cell combines with a
failing one), *what durations must be representable* is decided by the transit deadline/window
semantics, and *which resolution* is decided by the measurement that revision 1 scheduled after the
freeze. Encoding is the last step of a semantic design, not the first.

**The headline measurement could not fail in the way that mattered.** Revision 1 proposed to compare
our pipeline against Valhalla `sources_to_targets` and call passing that "decision 12 tested". Both
sides are the same engine: it measures whether we reconstruct Valhalla faithfully, which is worth
knowing and is *not* whether a household's commute estimate is accurate. Memo §0 correction 9
already withdrew exactly this conflation once. Revision 1 reintroduced it with a sampling design
attached.

**A real bug was hiding behind the word "exactly".** Memo D1 says `expansion.py` "reconstructs both
endpoint times exactly". Codex produced a counterexample, since reproduced here and fixed: for a
root edge, the adapter *assumed* the stored geometry ran away from the search origin. Where it runs
toward the origin instead, the destination received a full edge traversal and a node 39 m away
received 0 s — the two endpoint times swapped, at the destination, where the surface must be
most accurate. A second defect in the same function turned any edge naming an absent predecessor
into a fabricated time-zero root.

Worse than the bug is what finding it revealed: **`expansion.py` has never been run against a real
Valhalla response.** Nothing outside the unit tests calls it, and
`spikes/g1_expansion_primitive.py:47` requests only `duration` and `distance` — not the `edge_id`
and `pred_edge_id` the adapter needs — then does its own whole-edge charging. So the measured
expansion result in `g1-routing.md` (median 69 s, p90 271 s, the +66 s bias) describes a *different*
implementation from the one decision D1 adopted, and the synthetic fixtures that did exercise the
adapter all happened to use the favourable orientation.

Two further corrections to revision 1's own text, both found by checking the repository rather than
trusting the draft:

- It quoted listings leakage as median 3.12× and max 5.45× from `g4-listings.md`, **a file that
  marks itself superseded.** The replacement, `g4-listings-triangle.md`, reports **median 3.47× and
  max 6.50×**, with five disconnected parts in its first row. The memo flagged that supersession and
  revision 1 cited the stale file anyway.
- It called buildings "the real denominator" for coverage. Overture building footprints include
  garages, sheds and commercial structures, and one apartment block is many households. The correct
  claim is **building-weighted coverage**, which is a better diagnostic than cell coverage and is
  still not a count of homes.

Stages run in this order because each decides something the next needs. Within a stage, tasks are
parallel unless stated.

| stage | decides | gates |
|---|---|---|
| **S0 Semantics** | what a cell value *means*, what statuses exist, how criteria combine, what identifies a transit query | everything; no code freeze before it |
| **S1 Primitive** | whether the adopted drive/bike primitive is correct on real payloads | S3, and any drive or bike layer |

problems early. It may not freeze any semantics, and nothing it displays may be described as
validated until S3 and S7 land.

## S0 — Semantics, before any encoding

**Files:** create `docs/plans/layer-contract.md`, `v2/locfinder/status.py`, `v2/tests/test_status.py`.

Revision 1 proposed five sentinel codes. The codes are not the hard part; **producing them is.**
`layer.py:29` and `snap.py:183` both collapse several distinct causes into a single `inf`, and a
cutoff-bounded search cannot in general distinguish "no path exists" from "not reached within this
horizon". Allocating a code for a distinction the pipeline cannot make would publish a fiction.

**S0.1 Status provenance.** Enumerate the causes a cell can have no time — off-network beyond the
access radius, outside the routing bbox, searched and unreachable, not reached within the built
horizon, source missing — and decide for each whether the current pipeline can *produce* it. Carry
the cause from acquisition and routing through snapping instead of discarding it at the first `inf`.
Where the pipeline genuinely cannot distinguish, the published status is the weaker, honest one:
**"not reached within the built horizon"**, never "unreachable".

**S0.2 The algebra truth table.** Three outcomes per cell, and the precedence rule matters more than
the codes. Codex's correction to revision 1: a cell with **one known failure** and one unknown
criterion is **excluded**, not unknown — a known disqualification is not erased by an unrelated gap.
Unknown applies only when no criterion fails and at least one is unknown. Write the table, including
what happens when a user's threshold exceeds the built horizon (which requires a fresh computation,
not an inference from the published surface).

**S0.3 Per-anchor semantics.** The goal says "each of two workplaces". `min(t_A, t_B)` means
*either* workplace and is what category layers compute; per-anchor AND is what a household means and
is a different query. Specify both, and specify that a category criterion is "any qualifying
destination" while an anchor criterion is "this specific place".

**S0.4 Transit query identity.** A transit surface is not identified by `(mode, category)`. Fix the
service date, timezone, deadline, window, DST and duration semantics now, because they become
manifest fields and cache-key components in S4 — and because A6's freshness gate cannot be written
against an unspecified "requested service date".

**S0.5 Engines, stated per mode.** Walking from our own graph, driving and cycling from Valhalla
expansion, transit from MOTIS. Revision 1's architecture paragraph named only Valhalla and MOTIS
while other sections relied on the own-graph walk path. One statement, in one place.

**Pass criteria.** A written contract; `status.py` implementing only statuses the pipeline can
actually produce; a truth-table test per row including the known-failure-beats-unknown precedence;
no encoding chosen yet.

## S1 — Repair and verify the primitive

**S1.1 The expansion adapter fix — done 2026-11-03, needs real payloads.**
`locfinder/traveltime/expansion.py` now *derives* a root edge's orientation instead of assuming it:
from the supplied search origins when available (authoritative — the near end of a root is the
origin), otherwise from the vertex it shares with its successors (the search left through the far
end), and it **raises** rather than guessing when neither signal exists. An edge naming an absent
predecessor no longer becomes a time-zero root; it is dropped and counted in an out-parameter
`report=`. Six new tests, including the reproduced counterexample; 118 passing.

**S1.2 Request the fields the adapter needs, and run it on real data.** Add `edge_id` and
`pred_edge_id` to the `expansion_properties` request in `spikes/g1_expansion_primitive.py:47`, pass
`origins=` (the category's POI coordinates), and route the comparison through `expansion.py` rather
than its own whole-edge helper. Report `report=` counts: if real responses contain many dangling
predecessors or unorientable roots, that is a finding about the payload, not a nuisance.

**S1.3 Structural validation before any aggregate benchmark.** Aggregate medians hid this bug once.
Validate on real payloads: root orientation in both geometry directions, interior anchors that split
an edge, one-ways, turn-dependent costing, and the symmetric-edge assumption
(`fwd_time == rev_time`, which the adapter asserts and directional costing could falsify).

**S1.4 Then the paired re-measurement.** 240+ cells across T = 10/15/25/45 against
`sources_to_targets`. Report median, p90, **signed** median and false in/exclusion per T.

**Pass criteria.** S1.3's structural checks pass before S1.4 runs. S1.4's signed median within
±15 s — the claimed bias removal. Note what this does and does not show: it is **construction
fidelity against the same engine**, which is a prerequisite for accuracy and not evidence of it.
Zero observed false inclusions on a finite sample is not proof of zero rate (see S3.3).

## S2 — Resolution and lawful access

**S2.1 The place name resolver — absent from revision 1 entirely.** Memo D4 says the metro-bounded
name index, candidate chooser and correction flow are **unbuilt**, and revision 1 began with an
"already resolved anchor". Census geocoding resolved **none** of ten place-name queries, and
unbounded Nominatim returned "UNC Hospitals" 180 km away. No amount of routability repairs a
workplace resolved to the wrong entity.
**Files:** create `v2/locfinder/places/resolve.py`, `v2/tests/test_resolve.py`.
Source-native identity, metro-bounded search, ambiguity surfaced as candidates, pin correction, then
routability. The measured failure cases are the fixtures.

**S2.2 Anchor routability and legal snapping.** The RDU stub. Snap, prove a path exists **for the
required direction** (arrival semantics: can the anchor be *reached*, which on one-ways is not the
same as reaching out from it), nudge to the nearest point that routes, cap the nudge, and return the
adjustment for display. Codex's addition: a geometric nudge can cross a fence while staying close,
so the nudge must respect barriers and legal connectors, not just distance. Reuse the same bounded
legal snapping for category entrances and transit stops.

**S2.3 The access audit frame.** Pass 6 left open whether the routes the access fix recovered are
**lawful**, which is not answered by asking whether they are residential. Audit the selected paths:
restricted endpoints versus through traffic, barrier tags, both reachability directions, and
transition counts in each direction against a **preserved paired baseline on frozen identical
inputs**. Codex checked and no matched before-array exists, so it must be regenerated, not
reconstructed from the layer inventory.

**S2.4 Building-weighted coverage.** 23.24% of cells have no walk time in the grocery/walk build
(253,679 finite of 330,502 — verified). Rasterise Overture building footprints and report coverage
weighted by building count **alongside** cell coverage, naming it building-weighted and not home
coverage (M1). List the ten largest contiguous no-data regions with building counts and give each a
named cause before Phase 1 ships. Add Overture **Buildings** to `source-registry.md` with its own
obligation set — the registry currently covers Places only.

**S2.5 Make the penalty sweep measure what we ship.** Fixed 2026-11-03: the sweep called
`cell_seconds_from_arrival` without `node_cost` or the final `cutoff_s`, so it measured a pipeline
we do not ship, which is why its table read 77.32% against the production rebuild's 76.8%. Re-run on
frozen inputs and supersede the table.

## S3 — Grid, encoding and what we may claim about accuracy

**S3.1 Candidate grids must be evaluated at matched locations.** Codex executed `TRIANGLE.grid()` at
both resolutions and they are **not nested**: 100 m origin 661900/4003500 (643 × 514), 200 m origin
661800/4003600 (322 × 258). Sampling each grid at its own cell centres would confound resolution with
alignment and location. Use **one set of residential coordinates and destination entrances
throughout**, and evaluate each candidate grid and encoding at those same points.

**S3.2 The decomposition, corrected.** Revision 1 claimed three separable components; encoding
appeared in two of them and "model error" bundled network, snapping, entrance and route-choice
differences together. Retain four **paired** values per sampled location and report signed paired
differences — never sums or medians of medians:

| value | what it is |
|---|---|
| exact route to the location | the engine's own answer at that coordinate |
| float surface at its cell centre | our reconstruction, pre-encoding |
| encoded surface | what the product actually ships |
| independent prediction | a different engine's opinion, matched arrival time |

The fourth is **engine disagreement**, not model error, until its causes are controlled.

**S3.3 The audit, with precision declared in advance.** Revision 1 chose 60 cells per side per
threshold with no estimand. Codex's arithmetic: with 60 independent observations and **zero**
observed failures the one-sided 95% upper bound on the rate is **4.87%**, not zero; roughly **385
observations per rate denominator** gives a five-point worst-case margin, and clustering raises it.
So predeclare: the estimand, the sampling frame, the band definition (spatial adjacency and one
encoding increment are different populations), strata and allocations, weights, confidence
intervals, and missing-data treatment. Treat 60 per side as a **pilot** whose output is a variance
estimate, not a verdict.

Boundary-only sampling is also insufficient on its own: a pipeline that wrongly deletes a
neighbourhood never samples that neighbourhood. Add an **independent residential sample** and a
**predicted-unreachable stratum**.

**S3.4 Three outcomes, not a pass/fail.** This is the honesty mechanism revision 1 got wrong by
labelling the engineering gate "decision 12":

1. **Construction fidelity: PASS / FAIL.** Our pipeline against exact routes from the same engine.
   Ours to fix; a failure is a defect.
2. **Engine disagreement: reported, with magnitude.** Descriptive.
3. **Real-world accuracy: supported / failed / UNVERIFIED.** Decision 12 stays **unverified** until
   independent observations support it. Revision 1's assumptions table required *both* Google
   disagreement *and* confirming owner journeys before weakening the claim, which made an untaken or
   inconclusive journey an escape hatch. One condition, stated plainly: absent independent
   observation, the claim is unverified and the label says so.

Shipping with an unverified accuracy claim is a legitimate product decision for a friends-and-family
release. It is the owner's to make explicitly, not something a measurement design should grant by
construction.

**S3.5 Then freeze the encoding.** Only now, with S0's statuses and S3.1's measurements in hand.
Carry forward from revision 1, corrected:

- **Legacy codes 251–254 are valid minute values today**, verified. Any sentinel scheme must
  **version the format** and rebuild or namespace existing artifacts; it may not reinterpret them.
- **The writer, exporter and browser decoder change together.** `grid.py:76` fixes dtype to uint8,
  `layer.py:61` uses that profile, and `g3-render/worker.js:144` recognises exactly one sentinel. A
  Python round-trip test cannot protect the browser; test actual browser decoding. Categorical
  status codes must never be interpolated during reprojection.
- **State bounds as bounds.** Whole-minute rounding contributes *up to* 30 s; that is a worst case at
  T = 10, not a routine half of the budget. Half-minutes still round; integer seconds are not exact
  for fractional inputs.
- Define the **supported maximum duration** first (it follows from S0.4's transit horizon and the
  relaxation limits), then choose a resolution that covers it. For reference, the current Triangle
  walk layer is **174 KB** deflated at uint8 whole minutes with 121 distinct values and a maximum of
  exactly 120 — the cutoff — so a cell *at* the horizon is today indistinguishable from a genuine
  120-minute answer.
- Measure compressed transfer size **and phone decode latency** per candidate, not just size.

## S4 — Reproducible builds and atomic publication

Revision 1 had a manifest and no builder, which Codex correctly called insufficient: acquisition
dates do not identify exact inputs and do not invalidate dependent outputs.

**S4.1 One build command** over source acquisition → graph and index preparation → layers →
validation → publication, keyed by input hashes and release IDs plus code, profile and category
versions. Two concrete existing hazards to close: `fetch_places.py:31` reuses an existing raw file
regardless of the requested release, after which `partition_by_licence` can label it with the
*requested* release; and `g1_expansion_primitive.py:100` loads comparison artifacts without a full
identity check.

**S4.2 Immutable bundles, atomic manifest switch, rollback, cache invalidation.**

**S4.3 The freshness gate** (D7, still unimplemented) inside that path: sources declare acquisition
date and validity; GTFS validates against S0.4's service date with a non-zero active-trip
assertion, including exceptions, overnight service and DST; a download failure is a hard error.
Duke Transit's feed (calendar ended 2025-08-10) is the fixture; Chapel Hill's 2099 end date is the
second, showing a distant end date proves nothing. **Decide the policy explicitly:** the adopted
seven-feed configuration *includes* the stale Duke feed, so the choice is between blocking all
transit and excluding Duke with a declared partial-coverage statement. Revision 1 implied both.

**S4.4 Publication obligations.** `licence.py` returns only the first resolved licence, while memo
D3 requires retaining the **validated set** of per-source obligations. Carry required notices
through to publication, add registry entries for Buildings and county inventories, and test that
contractually restricted identifiers (Google `place_id`, RentCast, IDX, portal data) **cannot** enter
the ODbL bundle. Keep OSM contribution evidence independent of Overture and Google.

**S4.5 Minimal operations.** Health, stale-data, failure, memory and request-cost monitoring for the
single VPS.

## S5 — The vertical slice

**S5.1** One resolved anchor (S2.1) → one drive surface through the frozen contract → one criterion
card → rendered region → one listings deep link → explicit failure states. Ends when someone who is
not us can run it unaided.

**S5.2 The on-demand path must match the precomputed one.** `g2_anchor_bench.py:28` benchmarks
polygon `/isochrone`, but the product needs an encoded grid, and G1 already rejected the raster
approximation at threshold accuracy. Specify the production grid endpoint and add **paired
online/precomputed consistency tests**: selecting a named destination through the two interfaces must
not visibly move its boundary. Benchmark complete cache **misses** through encoding and transfer, not
only hits.

**S5.3 Cache identity.** Keyed by grid, source and profile versions, direction, and transit
date/deadline — not coordinate alone. Bound and measure how far coordinate rounding may move an
anchor, since it can cross a divided road or a barrier.

**S5.4 Owner gates, completed and recorded here**, because synthesis §6 wants them before heavy
architecture: the G4 device matrix, the G3 phone render, 3–5 G5a household sessions, and the
hand-timed journeys. Revision 1 only *scheduled* G5a and treated scheduling as the deliverable.

**S5.5 Declare A8's targets before running it.** "Report throughput and cache hit rate" cannot fail.
Name the workload, the minimum throughput, the latency and memory limits, and the failure taxonomy.

## S6 — Breadth

**S6.1 Two workplace cards plus an amenity and an airport criterion**, with S0.3's per-anchor AND
semantics, mixed-grid alignment handled, and the **combined region** audited directly. Individually
acceptable per-criterion error distributions do not establish the accuracy of their intersection,
and p90s do not compose by arithmetic.

**S6.2 "Why is this out?"** lists every failing constraint, never the first.

**S6.3 Categories, defined and validated per category.** Current selectors test taxonomy and tags
only — not opening status, public eligibility or usable access. Publish user-visible category
definitions with their source and eligibility rules. For parks: reconcile Overture 363 against OSM
545 (only 63 agreeing within 50 m) with county inventories, and model **entrances** rather than
centroids — while distinguishing *verified* entrances from *inferred* candidates, since a walk edge
crossing a park boundary may be fenced or grade-separated. Audit **precision as well as recall**;
county inventories are not automatically complete across municipal and state parks. A9 gates only
the categories it touches, so unvalidated categories stay unpublished rather than blocking the rest.

**S6.4 The transit surface.** MOTIS `one-to-all` returns stops; a cell surface needs the legal street
access leg (S2.2), a **direct-walk alternative taken as a minimum** — the Cameron Village pair
returns no itinerary because the query asks only for transit, though that alone does not prove the
cause, so define whether transit mode permits walk-only journeys and with what limit — and S0.4's
deadline policy. Test cell-to-stop composition against **full itineraries**, including non-nearest
stops, transfers and final egress. Ten timetable checks are a schedule check, not a reliability
validation. Measure the 90-minute cap at its boundary and sample import and query memory **peaks**;
117 MiB is idle.

**S6.5** Versioned share URLs (full query per owner decision 5; version the format so redaction can
be added later without breaking links). Listings handoff citing `g4-listings-triangle.md`'s **3.47×
median, 6.50× maximum**, and computing leakage for the **user's actual rendered region** rather than
quoting experimental aggregates.

## S7 — Release acceptance

Frozen-input regression tests (a golden region with a declared metric and an explicit
baseline-change policy — a legitimate road refresh *should* move a boundary, and a wrong baseline can
be perfectly stable) kept separate from source-refresh review and from independent accuracy tests.
Combined-region correctness, online/precomputed consistency, and a written statement of every
accuracy claim that remains **unverified**.

## Assumptions, and what would invalidate them

| assumption | invalidated if | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Construction fidelity is achievable to tolerance | S1.4 or S3.4(1) fails | the primitive is wrong; no label fixes it |
| A static model is adequate for a relocation decision | independent observations show otherwise | a departure-time-aware tier, or a weaker claim — **the claim is unverified until then regardless** |
| No-data regions are mostly unpopulated | S2.4 finds buildings in them | a network or access defect, ahead of all S5 work |
| One 8 GB VPS serves the on-demand tier | S5.5 misses its declared targets | 4 vCPU; the deferred budget decision returns |
| Browser-side algebra is fast enough on a phone | S5.4's phone run fails | server-rendered mask, already identified |
| Valhalla's bike profile is sound | untested — the spot check is **drive-only** | a bike calibration task returns; dropping our own unused bike fallback did not validate Valhalla's |

**Priors, labelled as such** (not measured here): one vertical slice before breadth is an
implementation prior, not an established ordering; the 450/120 s access penalties are Valhalla
defaults; hand-timed journeys are valuable independent observations but cannot certify a boundary
p90, and are not the only possible independent source.
