## 0. What changed from revision 1, and why

Revision 1 put an encoding freeze first and a boundary audit second. Both were wrong, and the
reasons are worth keeping because they are the same reasons the Phase 0 write-ups needed six
correction passes.

**The ordering was circular.** Revision 1's A1 froze a sentinel scheme and a numeric resolution,
but *what statuses exist* is decided by the constraint algebra (how an unknown cell combines with a
failing one), *what durations must be representable* is decided by the transit deadline/window
semantics, and *which resolution* is decided by the measurement that revision 1 scheduled after the
freeze. Encoding is the last step of a semantic design, not the first.

**The headline measurement could not fail in the way that mattered.** Revision 1 proposed to compare
our pipeline against Valhalla `sources_to_targets` and call passing that "decision 12 tested". Both
sides are the same engine: it measures whether we reconstruct Valhalla faithfully, which is worth
knowing and is *not* whether a household's commute estimate is accurate. Memo §0 correction 9
already withdrew exactly this conflation once. Revision 1 reintroduced it with a sampling design
attached.

**A real bug was hiding behind the word "exactly".** Memo D1 says `expansion.py` "reconstructs both
endpoint times exactly". Codex produced a counterexample, since reproduced here and fixed: for a
root edge, the adapter *assumed* the stored geometry ran away from the search origin. Where it runs
toward the origin instead, the destination received a full edge traversal and a node 39 m away
received 0 s — the two endpoint times swapped, at the destination, where the surface must be
most accurate. A second defect in the same function turned any edge naming an absent predecessor
into a fabricated time-zero root.

Worse than the bug is what finding it revealed: **`expansion.py` has never been run against a real
Valhalla response.** Nothing outside the unit tests calls it, and
`spikes/g1_expansion_primitive.py:47` requests only `duration` and `distance` — not the `edge_id`
and `pred_edge_id` the adapter needs — then does its own whole-edge charging. So the measured
expansion result in `g1-routing.md` (median 69 s, p90 271 s, the +66 s bias) describes a *different*
implementation from the one decision D1 adopted, and the synthetic fixtures that did exercise the
adapter all happened to use the favourable orientation.

Two further corrections to revision 1's own text, both found by checking the repository rather than
trusting the draft:

- It quoted listings leakage as median 3.12× and max 5.45× from `g4-listings.md`, **a file that
  marks itself superseded.** The replacement, `g4-listings-triangle.md`, reports **median 3.47× and
  max 6.50×**, with five disconnected parts in its first row. The memo flagged that supersession and
  revision 1 cited the stale file anyway.
- It called buildings "the real denominator" for coverage. Overture building footprints include
  garages, sheds and commercial structures, and one apartment block is many households. The correct
  claim is **building-weighted coverage**, which is a better diagnostic than cell coverage and is
  still not a count of homes.

## S0 — Semantics, before any encoding

**Files:** create `docs/plans/layer-contract.md`, `v2/locfinder/status.py`, `v2/tests/test_status.py`.

Revision 1 proposed five sentinel codes. The codes are not the hard part; **producing them is.**
`layer.py:29` and `snap.py:183` both collapse several distinct causes into a single `inf`, and a
cutoff-bounded search cannot in general distinguish "no path exists" from "not reached within this
horizon". Allocating a code for a distinction the pipeline cannot make would publish a fiction.

**S0.1 Status provenance.** Enumerate the causes a cell can have no time — off-network beyond the
access radius, outside the routing bbox, searched and unreachable, not reached within the built
horizon, source missing — and decide for each whether the current pipeline can *produce* it. Carry
the cause from acquisition and routing through snapping instead of discarding it at the first `inf`.
Where the pipeline genuinely cannot distinguish, the published status is the weaker, honest one:
**"not reached within the built horizon"**, never "unreachable".

**S0.2 The algebra truth table.** Three outcomes per cell, and the precedence rule matters more than
the codes. Codex's correction to revision 1: a cell with **one known failure** and one unknown
criterion is **excluded**, not unknown — a known disqualification is not erased by an unrelated gap.
Unknown applies only when no criterion fails and at least one is unknown. Write the table, including
what happens when a user's threshold exceeds the built horizon (which requires a fresh computation,
not an inference from the published surface).

**S0.3 Per-anchor semantics.** The goal says "each of two workplaces". `min(t_A, t_B)` means
*either* workplace and is what category layers compute; per-anchor AND is what a household means and
is a different query. Specify both, and specify that a category criterion is "any qualifying
destination" while an anchor criterion is "this specific place".

**S0.4 Transit query identity.** A transit surface is not identified by `(mode, category)`. Fix the
service date, timezone, deadline, window, DST and duration semantics now, because they become
manifest fields and cache-key components in S4 — and because A6's freshness gate cannot be written
against an unspecified "requested service date".

**S0.5 Engines, stated per mode.** Walking from our own graph, driving and cycling from Valhalla
expansion, transit from MOTIS. Revision 1's architecture paragraph named only Valhalla and MOTIS
while other sections relied on the own-graph walk path. One statement, in one place.

**Pass criteria.** A written contract; `status.py` implementing only statuses the pipeline can
actually produce; a truth-table test per row including the known-failure-beats-unknown precedence;
no encoding chosen yet.

## S1 — Repair and verify the primitive — **done 2026-11-04, revised after Codex pass 9**

Results: `../experiments/g1-routing.md`. Review: `../research/16-codex-adversarial-review-pass9-s1-primitive.md`
(4 High, 4 Medium, no Critical). Code: `locfinder/traveltime/expansion.py`; spikes
`s1_expansion_structure.py`, `s1_expansion_directions.py`, `s1_expansion_node_fidelity.py`,
`s1_access_leg_decomposition.py`, `g1_expansion_primitive.py` (now the re-measurement).

**S1.1 Root handling — rewritten twice.** Pass 7 found that a root's orientation was *assumed*;
pass 9 found that orienting a root at all is the wrong frame. **A root is a partial edge.** The
search origin sits *inside* the road and Valhalla does not split the edge in its output, so neither
endpoint is the near end. The consequences, all measured:

- time zero landed on a vertex that a matrix query from that very node answered in **2-5 s**, not 0;
- both opposing roots of one road picked the *same* nearer endpoint, were re-expressed into the
  same travel direction and merged into one record — **deleting a legal direction**;
- a partial duration was interpolated across a full road.

Now the root is **split at the origin's projection**: a virtual node there carries time 0, the
root's `duration` is the traversal time of exactly the half that was traversed, and the two
opposing roots become two sub-edges meeting at that node. On the real drive payload **5 of 6 roots
split**, and two origins now project at fraction 0.0 of their sub-edge instead of 0.6.

Pass 9 also **inverted the orientation priority**. The successor vertex is topological — read off
the response's own tree — while origin proximity is geometric and the origin is a POI 11-26 m off
the road. Two of six real roots had proximity contradict an unambiguous successor signal, so
successors now win and proximity is a recorded fallback (`roots_oriented_by_proximity`, **0** on
the real payload).

**S1.2 The fields, and the first real payload — done.** `edge_id`/`pred_edge_id` requested,
`origins=` passed, comparison routed through `expansion.py`, `cost` requested too.

**S1.3 Structural validation — done; six findings, all now in the code or the report.**

1. **`skip_opposites` must be `false`.** With it on, 636 of 254,323 drive edges (0.25%) name a
   predecessor Valhalla never serialised. With it off: **0**. Cost: 476,072 features, 2.4 s.
2. **The two directed features of one road were never merged.** Measured with node times and node
   identities held fixed: **15.65% of cells change, median −4.7 s, 99.7% of them earlier.**
3. **One-way edges were marked bidirectional.** **9.17% of cells change, median +2.5 s, 100% of
   them later**, with 72 cells losing reachability. 12.7% of physical edges are one-way-only.
4. **`skip_opposites=true` does not suppress every opposite** (664 pairs survive), so it cannot be
   used to infer the flag. `opposites_included` is a required keyword with no default.
5. **A key groups a shape, not a topological edge.** On the real payload **519 groups hold more
   than two features and the largest holds five** — four distinct directed edge IDs collapsing into
   one record. Whether those are different roads, hierarchy levels or turn states cannot be
   determined without tile attribution, so the adapter now **reports** the count
   (`groups_over_two_features`) and claims no identity. Pairing on authoritative opposing-edge IDs
   is the real fix and is not available from `/expansion`.
6. **A closed loop defeated the order test.** Both stored directions of a loop start at the same
   vertex, so an endpoint-only comparison always said "same order" and merged a loop with its
   reverse into one direction. Now the whole coordinate sequence is compared. No exact closed
   geometry appeared in the live drive response, so this is a repaired latent defect, not a
   measured incidence.

Carried forward, not fixed: `_node_key` is a square quantisation bucket, not a distance test — it
can merge endpoints 0.7 m apart and separate endpoints 0.02 m apart (live maximum spread within a
conflated bucket: 0.38 m, and none of the sampled nodes were affected); `tau` contains turn and stop
costs, which interpolation scales (median implied drive speed **3.17 m/s**); Valhalla's `cost`
exceeds its `duration` on **100%** of drive features (median cumulative gap 264 s), so carrying cost
as a selection weight is a named follow-up.

**S1.4 The paired re-measurement. The reconstruction agrees with the engine at the median for walk,
and is 50 s conservative for drive.**

### The drive gap is destination selection, and the "biased primitive" framing is withdrawn

The gap survived the root fix and reproduced with no grid at all, so it is not the snapping. I
proposed auto costing's turn and stop penalties as the mechanism and labelled it a hypothesis.
**The hypothesis was wrong**, and the measurement that settled it is simple: vary the number of
destinations.

| mode | POIs | n | tree − matrix (median) |
|---|---|---|---|
| drive | **1** | 53 | **+0.0 s** |
| drive | 3 | 54 | **+131 s** |
| walk | 1 | 56 | +11.5 s |
| walk | 3 | 51 | +6.0 s |

`/expansion` is a **multi-source** reverse search. For a node it reports the time along the
**cost**-optimal path to whichever POI is *cost*-nearest; the matrix minimum reports the *smallest
time* over the per-pair cost-optimal paths. Those differ exactly when the cost-nearest destination
is not the time-nearest one. With one destination there is nothing to select between and the drive
gap is **zero**. Walk barely moves at any count, because for pedestrians cost and duration nearly
coincide — while Valhalla's `cost` exceeds its `duration` on **100%** of drive features, median
cumulative 264 s, which is what makes the two selections diverge there.

Three controls rule out the alternatives: `/route` and `sources_to_targets` agree with each other
to **+0.6 s** (p90 0.9 s), so the reference was not the confound; the gap persists where the tree
and the router agree on path *length* (+206 s), so it is not path choice within a pair; and zeroing
the penalties halves it rather than removing it, which is expected since penalties drive both the
cost-to-duration divergence and route choice, and therefore cannot separate them.

**So the primitive is faithful and the earlier framing was mine to withdraw.** This is the *same
phenomenon as Codex pass-8 H2*, one level up: a surface built from a multi-destination search holds
the time to the cost-nearest destination, so it is an **upper bound** on `min_d t(cell → d)`, not
that minimum. The two findings are one, and S3 should carry them as one.

The product consequence survives, with a sharper boundary than before:

- an **anchor** criterion has one destination, so a drive anchor surface is faithful;
- a **category** criterion is a multi-destination search, so a drive category surface over-reports
  and can produce false exclusions, never false inclusions. The 10-minute threshold figure (5 of 12
  reference positives missed) is a consequence of *this*, not of a biased reconstruction.

**"Exact" was the wrong word, and that was my error.** A near-zero signed median bounds no
individual cell. The walk node comparison has p90 **30 s** and a maximum of **632 s**, with 17.1%
of nodes later and 14.6% earlier by more than a second; the cell comparison's on-network residual
has its own tail. The defensible claim is **median agreement with heavy tails**, and the tails are
unexplained.

**The threshold figures are conditional, not rates.** The earlier "41.7% false exclusions at a
10-minute drive threshold" is **5 of 12 reference-positive cells in a 240-cell sample** — and that
sample is drawn only from the model's own reachable set under the horizon, so a truly-close cell the
model cannot reach has *zero probability of being sampled*. The reference-positive counts are 12-23
at the short thresholds. Direction and magnitude for the full grid are unquantified; S3's sampling
frame must give positive inclusion probability outside the model's reachable set.

**Pass criteria, honestly.** S1.3's checks ran before S1.4 and changed the code twice. S1.4's
"signed median within ±15 s" **fails as written** for both modes, because it compares our cell value
(which includes an access leg) against a reference that has none. On the on-network component: walk
**passes** at +0.0 s, drive **fails** at +50 s. A failed construction criterion does not become a
pass by reinterpretation, so both verdicts are published.

**Consequences for the plan.** D1 adopted expansion for drive and bike on the superseded numbers,
and the re-measurement supports the adoption: the reconstruction is faithful. What S3 must carry
separately are two terms that are *not* reconstruction error — our access-leg model, and the
cost-versus-time destination selection that makes any category surface an upper bound. Walk is
unaffected by the second (cost ≈ duration) and dominated by the first. Bike is untested and
inherits both questions. The remaining genuine unknown is the **tails**: walk's node-level p90 is
30 s with a maximum of 632 s, and that is unexplained.

**`strict` is not a validity gate**, and must not be adopted as one. It checks missing predecessors,
unorientable roots and (now) decreasing cumulative durations; it does not check graph identity, root
coverage, duplicate IDs or endpoint-join quality. One root of six still cannot be oriented, so
`strict=True` fails on the real payload; production needs `strict=False` plus an explicit acceptance
check, and `dangling_predecessors == 0` is not that check.

**A correction to a Phase 0 conclusion.** `g1-routing.md` called our walk model "not merely
acceptable, it is unbiased" on a signed median of +0.2 min. Decomposed by access distance that is a
*net of two opposing terms*: about **10 s of on-network optimism** and a conservative access charge
reaching +97 s in the 100-200 m band, cancelling near the sample's median access distance of 31 m.
The cancellation is a property of the sample, not the model. The verdict's direction survives — under
1% at a median 22-minute journey — but "unbiased" overstates it, and `ACCESS_SPEED_FACTOR` is now the
larger of the two terms and remains an unmeasured prior.

**Two errors of mine, recorded.** (1) `matrix_*.json` stores **minutes** (`own_min` means "own, in
minutes", not "own minimum") while `raster_primitive_*.json` stores seconds; mixing them gave an
access-charge-to-bias ratio of 121.6, and that implausible number is what exposed it. (2) The S1.4
report template asserted "the remaining bias is our own access-leg model" **unconditionally**, so it
printed that sentence for drive immediately after printing drive's on-network FAIL. The conclusion
is now derived from the result.

**S2.1 The place name resolver — absent from revision 1 entirely.** Memo D4 says the metro-bounded
name index, candidate chooser and correction flow are **unbuilt**, and revision 1 began with an

**Built 2026-11-04** (`locfinder/places/resolve.py`). Real index: **113,720 places** from

legitimately sit outside the area we grid. 229 distinct localities. Ten verbatim benchmark queries:

Those figures are the **re-acquired** ones. The resolver was documented as bounding by the routing

and done**: 90,349 → **107,484** accepted places (+19%), 177 → **229** localities, and the index
grew 96,585 → 113,720. Still zero rows quarantined.

**But candidates are not correctness, and grading them separately changed the verdict.**
`spikes/s2_resolver_correctness.py` checks the answers against the independent spot-check reference
points at a deliberately coarse 800 m (the references are not POI centroids, so this separates
*same place* from *somewhere else*). Only five queries have a written-down intended answer and can
be graded at all:

| query | answer | verdict |
|---|---|---|
| Carrboro Farmers Market | "Carrboro Farmers Market", 227 m, `farmers_market` | **correct** |
| Southpoint | "Southpoint", 732 m — category **`dessert_shop`** | passes the distance test *for the wrong reason*: a tenant inside the mall, not the mall. Codex verified the address against Crumbl's own store page, and the mall itself is in the index at rank 16 |
| Duke Hospital | "Duke Hospital", 56 m — category **`platform`** | **wrong entity.** `node/6509747409` is a bus platform (`highway=bus_stop`, `public_transport=platform`, `network=Duke`) standing at the hospital. A distance test calls this correct, which is why identity is now checked separately |

So **one clearly correct, one accidentally near, three wrong** — against a headline of "7 single
answers". The gap between those two framings is the finding, and Codex pass-10 H1 sharpened it by
checking each answer against the operator's own published address rather than against a distance:
four of the seven are the intended entity, two are the wrong entity, one (Fidelity) depends on

The cause is visible in the table: an **exact match on a short or generic name beats a partial match

The `RTP` failure is separately a **coverage** gap: no place carries that alias, and the index is

wrong rather than incomplete. `Duke Hospital` and `UNC Hospitals` are a *naming* gap of the same
family: the hospital entity is not in the index under the name a household types, so no amount of
ranking reaches it.

**And the coverage gain was reported as a net, which hid a loss** (Codex pass-11 M3). I had written

not a property, and I implied it was one.** Codex pass-12 searched further and found cell (126, 612)

independently: the lost cell's snap distance is unchanged, its selected cost *falls* from 7,147.86
to 7,127.05 while its time *rises* from 7,027.86 to 7,127.05 s. The practical rule this sets: a
coverage change between two destination sets is reported as gained-and-lost, never as a net, and a
clean sweep of the thresholds we happen to test is not evidence about the ones we do not.

**That fix is narrower than it first sounds** (Codex pass-11 M2). It makes the *POI prefix* stable
for an unchanged id set; it does not make results rebuild-independent. `graph_to_cost_time_csr`
keeps the first of several equal-cost parallel edges and `EdgeIndex.from_graph` uses the same
strict-less-than rule, so insertion order still decides which *time* an equal-cost edge reports —
Codex demonstrated 10 s versus 90 s from the same pair in reverse order. `arrival.py` already
records that scipy's equal-cost path choice is insertion-order dependent, and `ox.nearest_nodes`
has no stable tie rule either. Nor does sorting preserve "the first three" across an *enlarged* id
set: a new lower id legitimately changes the cohort, so comparing the same three destinations needs
frozen ids, not a sort. Canonical node ordering, explicit tie policies and recording the selected
destination ids in each artifact are **S4.1** work.

**S2.2 Anchor routability and legal snapping — done 2026-11-04.**
`locfinder/places/anchor.py`, `tests/test_anchor.py` (15 tests), `spikes/s2_anchor_routability.py`.

`CoreReachability` implements the test `components.py` says is the real one: a candidate node is
usable as a *destination* exactly when a directed path runs **from** the largest strongly connected
component **to** it. One `O(V+E)` precomputation per graph (0.0 s on 438,250 nodes), constant-time
per anchor. Direction is the part most easily got backwards — a one-way *source* can only be left,
so nothing can drive to it, while a one-way *sink* is a perfectly good destination, and weak
connectivity calls both connected.

**What the directed test actually buys, measured — and it is a null result on this anchor set.**
On the Triangle drive graph, pruning to the largest weak component keeps 437,615 nodes, of which
**143 (0.0327%) cannot be reached from the core**. On the twelve spot-check anchors it changed the
chosen node **zero times**.

An earlier version of this section claimed "once", from UNC Hospitals. That was wrong, and fixing
the `nudged` miscalibration exposed it: the unreachable node near UNC Hospitals is **further away**
than the one chosen, so rejecting it adjusted nothing. The test is correct and essentially free
(0.0 s over 438,250 nodes), and it is worth keeping because the failure it prevents is silent — but
it has **not** been shown to fire on a real anchor, and saying otherwise oversold it.

**The RDU stub failure does not reproduce, and that is the honest finding.** All six nodes within
150 m of Terminal 2 are reachable from the core, so `largest_component` had already fixed the
Phase 0 failure; the directed test confirms it rather than rescuing it. The plan's framing of S2.2
around "the RDU stub" was out of date before the work started.

Result: **12 of 12 anchors usable, 0 nudged**, displacements 3.6-77.6 m. One anchor has an
unreachable candidate somewhere in range, which is not an adjustment and is reported separately.

**Two defects were mine, and the real payload found both within one run.**

1. **A barrier veto made a legitimate anchor unusable.** The first version rejected any candidate
   whose straight segment crossed a barrier, and RDU Terminal 2 came back `unreachable` — not for
   lack of reachability but because **all six** core-reachable candidates are cut off by the airport
   perimeter `barrier=fence`. The straight segment is a crude proxy for an approach that really
   loops round the terminal road, and at any fenced site — airport, campus, hospital, gated
   development — a veto is a false negative. A barrier now **ranks** rather than vetoes: prefer a
   candidate that crosses nothing, otherwise take the nearest reachable one and set
   `crossed_barrier` so the caveat travels with the answer.
2. **`nudged` meant nothing.** Defined as "moved more than a metre", it fired on **all twelve**
   anchors, because every POI is more than a metre from a road node. It now means a *nearer*
   candidate was rejected; `moved_m` still reports the displacement for display, which is what this
   task asked for.

Barriers are extracted from the routing extract with QuackOSM (4,665 separating ways):
`fence`, `wall`, `retaining_wall`, `hedge`, `city_wall`. **`kerb` (1,113 ways), `guard_rail` (350),
`handrail` and `ditch` are excluded** along with gates, bollards and cycle barriers — they are
crossable, often mode-dependently, and counting them as walls rejects legitimate approaches.

Reused unchanged for category entrances and transit stops in S6.3 and S6.4, which is why the API
takes an `EdgeIndex` rather than anything anchor-specific. Still open: the barrier test is
straight-line, so `crossed_barrier` marks an approach as un-validated rather than wrong, and a
genuine legal-connector check would need the approach path itself.

**S2.3 The access lawfulness audit — rebuilt and done 2026-11-04.** The first version's measurement
ran and reproduces; the conclusions drawn from it were withdrawn the same day after Codex pass 13,
and the rebuild is at the end of this section. Both are kept, because the withdrawn version is the
clearest record of how a plausible-looking audit can measure three things and conclude four.
`spikes/s2_access_lawfulness_audit.py`. Pass 6 left open whether the routes the access fix recovered
are **lawful**, which is not answered by asking whether they are residential. `access.py` lets a
restricted edge be *chosen* at a penalty rather than forbidden, which is what makes a home on a
private drive reachable; the risk is that the same mechanism lets a published route run **through**
private land, which `access=destination` forbids outright.

Restricted edges are identified as `cost - time > 0` on the retained matrices — the access penalty
and nothing else — so the audit tests the edges the search actually used rather than re-reading
tags. **117,253 of 1,395,589 edges (8.40%)** are restricted on the walk graph, all at the single
120 s walk penalty.

**Two numbers, and they answer different questions.** Both were overstated when first written, and
what each of them actually supports is recorded under "what pass 13 withdrew" below.

*An edge-position diagnostic.* Walking the predecessor forest and marking, per node, whether a
restricted edge appears anywhere after its own first hop and not as the final hop into a destination
(those two positions being where a driveway would sit):

| | nodes | share of reachable |
|---|---|---|
| reachable | 539,814 | — |
| first hop restricted — the driveway case, lawful | 48,865 | 9.05% |
| **shipped path routes through restricted land** | **26,395** | **4.89%** |

*Decisive — how much coverage has no lawful path at all.* A strict surface allowing a restricted
edge only as the first hop out of the origin, built through the production snap and **verified
cell-for-cell against S2.4's cached reason raster** before anything was concluded from it:

| | shipped | strict | lost |
|---|---|---|---|
| cells `OK` | 256,752 | 251,222 | 5,533 |
| buildings `OK` | 499,009 | 494,128 | **4,881 — 0.95%** |

12,042 reachable nodes lose every path; 25,186 keep one but get slower, by a median of **+703 s**.

### What pass 13 withdrew, and what S2.3 has to become

Codex reproduced every number above and then found four High defects that break what I concluded
from them. The numbers stand; the conclusions do not.

**Edge position does not establish lawfulness** (H3). Splitting one driveway into two edges flips a
journey's verdict without changing the journey, and being the first edge does not establish that a
private road serves *that* property or that the traveller may use it — `access=private` describes
restricted public access, not endpoint entitlement. So 26,395 / 4.89% is an **edge-position
diagnostic**: restricted use somewhere other than at either end. It is not a count of unlawful
routes, and I had called it one.

**The strict surface was not the policy it claimed**, in three separate ways:

- **H1, the policy is lost below the node level.** A cell halfway along an open edge inherits its
  node's restricted-first-hop route, so the restricted edge ends up *inside* the cell's journey.
  Codex's case: A→B open, B→C restricted, C→destination open, all 10 s; the node answers are
  correctly ∞ for A and 20 s for B, but a cell halfway along A→B gets 25 s.
- **H2, the "unpenalised" branch still charges penalties.** Both branches share one `EdgeIndex`,
  whose edge costs carry the access penalty; `node_cost=T_strict` changes only the node term. Codex's
  case reports 22.5 s where the permitted direct branch takes 17.5 s, which falsely loses the cell
  at a 20 s horizon.
- **H4, lawful alternatives are discarded before the policy is applied.** The filter runs on the
  CSR, i.e. *after* `graph_to_cost_time_csr` has collapsed parallel edges and kept the cost-minimal
  one. Where a restricted edge won that contest, removing it removes the pair entirely even though a
  slower public parallel edge exists. **27 directed node pairs** in the real graph, with **2,673**
  more where a restricted winner hides an open alternative.

Because those errors run in **both** directions, 0.95% is reproducible but is **not an upper bound**,
and the "omitting the destination-end hop makes it conservative" argument does not rescue it.

**The rebuild.** The policy to model is a **contiguous restricted chain from the origin**, not a
single exempt edge — that formulation is invariant to how a driveway is split, which is what kills
H3's artefact. It is exactly a **two-state product graph**: state 0 is still on the origin's
restricted chain and may traverse either kind of edge; leaving onto an open edge transitions
permanently to state 1, which may traverse open edges only. Solved as a two-state reverse Dijkstra
for arrival semantics. With that:

- filter the **multigraph** per policy *before* collapsing parallel edges, so H4 cannot happen;
- give the permitted state **time-valued** costs, so H2 cannot happen;
- carry both states through snapping, so an open partial edge continues into state 1 and H1 cannot
  happen.

### S2.3 rebuilt — done 2026-11-04

`locfinder/traveltime/policy.py` with `tests/test_policy.py`, driven by
`spikes/s2_access_lawfulness_v2.py`. Three states rather than two, because the policy is symmetric:
`R* O* R*` is an origin chain, an open middle and a destination chain. The language is its own
reverse, so an arrival search uses the same automaton with the chains exchanged. All four of Codex's
counterexamples are tests, plus two soundness invariants.

**One of those invariants caught a defect of mine before it became a number.** Comparing the lawful
time-minimum against the *shipped* surface made **26,376 nodes look faster under the policy than
without it**, which a subset of journeys cannot be. The shipped surface reports the time along the
**cost**-selected path — an upper bound on the minimum time (pass-8 H2) — so it is not a valid
baseline for anything that minimises time. `unrestricted_arrival` provides the right one. The lesson
generalises past this audit: **any counterfactual against the shipped surface is confounded by the
H2 bound**, and the two must be compared like with like.

| | value |
|---|---|
| directed pairs with **both** an open and a restricted edge | **2,748** |
| nodes with no journey in `R* O* R*` | **2,833 of 539,814 — 0.5248%** |
| nodes lawful but **more than one second** slower | 38,956; median **+12.2 s**, p90 +312.6 s, max +5,318.5 s |
| lawful faster than the baseline (must be 0) | **0** |
| buildings served within 120 min, no policy → under it | 507,037 → 504,945 |
| cost of the policy, **nearest-node proxy** | **2,092 buildings — 0.4126%** |
| split invariance, subdividing every restricted edge | finiteness identical, max diff **1.46e-11 s** (floating-point noise) |

The split-invariance line is the direct demonstration that H3 is closed: subdivision cannot change
the journey set, so a measure of journeys must not change, and it does not — to the bit.

**Reported against buildings, not cells, and H1 is the reason.** Carrying three automaton states
through the production cell snap is a real change to a tested module and the rule is subtle — a cell
reached along an *open* partial edge may continue only in states 0 or 1, while one on a *restricted*
partial edge may continue in any state. Rather than ship a second cell-level number I am unsure of,
the audit reports nodes completely and weights them by buildings assigned to their nearest node: a
building's own access leg is off-network, so all three states are admissible and the subtlety does
not arise. **No cell-level strict surface is claimed, and the earlier 5,533-cell figure is
withdrawn.**

**A production defect the audit surfaced, and re-measurement after it** (Codex pass-14 H3).
`speeds.symmetrise` added a walking reverse only when no edge existed between the two nodes *at
all*, so the presence of any `v -> u` edge suppressed the reverse of every `u -> v` road. Where
those are different roads of different access classes, a legitimate pedestrian route disappeared —
Codex's case has an **open** road at 100 s and a **restricted** one at 1 s between the same pair, so
the open road got no walkable reverse and a node behind it was unreachable under any policy
declining the restricted edge. This was in the **shipped walk graph**, not only in the audit, and
separating access classes downstream cannot recover an alternative already discarded upstream.
Reverses are now deduplicated per physical road, by `osmid` and length.

The figures above are measured **after** that fix. Its effect on them is small and worth stating as
such: both-class directed pairs **2,700 -> 2,748**, slowdowns 38,939 -> 38,956, median +12.3 ->
+12.2 s, and the headline 2,833 nodes and 2,092 buildings unchanged. The defect was real and the
fix is a correctness fix; it is not what moves this measurement.

**What this does not establish — and the claim I made twice and withdrew twice.** First I called
26,395 paths "unlawful"; pass 13 showed edge position cannot decide that. Then I said membership of
`R* O* R*` means a journey is "not through-traffic over private land"; **pass-14 H1 shows that is
also wrong.** An origin in estate A and a destination in estate C joined by a continuous restricted
road through *unrelated* estate B is `RRR` and is accepted, while B is being used for through
traffic. The automaton sees edge classes and cannot see whose chain an edge belongs to. Rejection is
no better: `ORO` can describe an entitled traveller using a connection they may use. And requiring
a non-empty open middle would reject a resident crossing their own estate.

So this measures **membership in an edge-class language** and nothing more. Two withdrawn framings
in two passes on one question is worth naming: the pull each time was to convert a computable
property into the product claim I wanted, and the computable property was never that claim.

**And the building figure is a nearest-node proxy** (pass-14 H2), not buildings served. Assigning a
building to its nearest node within 200 m assumes a connector that is zero-time, unrestricted and
traversable; the straight line may cross a fence, its travel time is not added — so a node at
7,190 s with a 100 m connector still counts inside a 120-minute horizon — and destination snapping
makes the same assumption at the other end. Claiming building *service* needs both endpoint
connectors modelled with their classes carried through the automaton.

Also reconciled while here: `access.py` documents 126,157 restricted edges; the current chain is
**126,197 raw → 127,426 symmetrised → 123,024 in the retained component → 117,253 in the CSR**. The
documented figure is historical, not wrong. 2,673 directed pairs hide a restricted alternative behind
an open winner, which is correct for describing *shipped selected* edges and wrong for a policy
counterfactual — the same H4 point from the other side.

**And this replaces a claim that was never supported** (Codex pass-12 M8). "The access fix is worth
1.7% of reachable cells (4,420 of 256,752)" reproduces, but it is a **length-keyed proximity
approximation**: a public and a restricted edge with equal rounded lengths are both classified
restricted, and a restricted edge whose projected length differs from its stored OSM length is
missed. More importantly it counts cells with a restricted edge *nearby*, not cells the intervention
recovered. Those two are not the same quantity and I had been using the first as the second. The
measured figure is the paired one above. Carrying edge identity through `EdgeIndex` so the
proximity statistic can be computed exactly is **S4.1**.

| licence | source entries | datasets |
|---|---|---|
| `ODbL-1.0` | 814,767 | OpenStreetMap 454,012; Microsoft ML Buildings 360,755 |

The decision: **widen the allowlist for this use and only this use.** `resolve_licence` now takes a

it is a decision rather than a proposal — with the standing condition that it must be revisited if
building footprints ever feed a published artifact, because a derived *database* containing ODbL
rows is share-alike.

Two things to carry forward. The CC BY string is **non-SPDX free text**, matched literally, so a
release that rewords it will quarantine again — the safe direction to fail, but it will look like a
regression. And `licence.py` had a latent defect this found: `row.get("sources") or []` raises on a
multi-element numpy array, which is exactly what GeoParquet provides; it survived only because the
single existing caller normalised to a list first. Fixed, with a regression test. A manifest-resolved
licence is now also checked against the allowlist, which it previously was not.

Those two percentages are **evaluation-extent**: 452 groceries. On the routing extent the same
production path reads **77.69%** at the adopted penalty. Both are correct for their own input, and
the sweep's conclusion is unchanged by the widening (Codex pass-11 M5).

Two results worth carrying forward, with the **routing-extent** figures (the narrower
evaluation-extent run is marked superseded in `../experiments/spotcheck-engines.md`). **The
unvalidated penalty prior barely moves what we publish**: across 0-960 s, coverage moves **0.09
points** (77.70% → 77.61%) and the median reported time 0.3 min, while the nodes whose path changes
go from 0 to **73,444** — so it is a route-choice knob, not a coverage one. That bounds the risk
from `access.py`'s 450/120 s priors for the own-graph **walk** layer only; drive uses Valhalla's own
penalties. **4,420 of 256,752 reachable cells have a restricted edge as their nearest**, of which
**4,178** are `access=private` — read that as an approximate *proximity* statistic, not as the
intervention's worth: it is length-keyed, so equal rounded lengths misclassify, and proximity is not
recovery (Codex pass-12 M8). S2.3's rebuilt measurement supersedes both: **2,833 of 539,814 nodes
(0.5248%) have no journey in `R* O* R*`**, with a nearest-node building proxy of 2,092. The
intermediate "5,533 cells and 4,881 buildings" figure was withdrawn with the rest of the first
audit (pass-13 H1-H4) and is not a measurement of anything.

## Codex pass 12 — the pass-11 fixes, and what they broke

`../research/19-codex-adversarial-review-pass12-fixes.md`. Four High, four Medium, one Low, all
reproduced with concrete inputs and all fixed. The paired destination measurement survived and was
verified more strictly than I had verified it: Codex checked the stored raw **bbox predicates**
select the same 452 ids, not merely that the count matched, and noted that "all 550 geometries are
points" was not sufficient proof on its own because their stored bboxes have non-zero width.

**The lock did not deliver what its docstring claimed, twice in a row** (H1). Pass 11 replaced "no
lock" with a lock file whose owner was its *content*, which cannot be released safely: staleness was
3600 s against an 1800 s wait, so a waiter broke a lock a live 45-minute download still held, and
the original holder's `finally` then unlinked the **replacement** lock and admitted a third process.
Codex reproduced all three ownership failures by ageing a held lock. The lock is now **OS-owned**
(`msvcrt.locking` / `fcntl.flock` on a handle held open for the duration) and the lock file is never
unlinked, because unlinking is the operation that caused the bug. Two new cross-process tests — an
OS lock is held per process, so a same-process re-entrant test could not have caught this and the
pass-11 test it replaced was testing the wrong thing.

**Three of the nine were my own over-corrections, in the same area, in three consecutive passes.**
Pass 10: equal *categories* before merging broke real de-duplication. Pass 11: a raw-set replacement
repeated it for `station`/`fuel`. Pass 12 (H4): the subsidiary-vs-parent boolean that replaced *that*
was too coarse the other way — `parking` and `fuel` are deliberately not subsidiary, so OSM's **RDU**
car park merged with Overture's **RDU** airport into one confidently resolved car park, and a
`pharmacy_counter` that fell through to `facility` began corroborating its own pharmacy. The rule is
now **equal roles**, which is coarse enough to span two taxonomies and fine enough that a car park
is not an airport. And M5 is an over-correction of pass-11 H4: the mixed-reference abstention made
`Franklin Street bus stop` ambiguous against a cafe it beat 1.000 to 0.830, so an explicit transport
word in the query now suppresses it.

The pattern is worth naming: **every one of these fixed the case in the review and broke an adjacent
case nobody had written down.** The protection that eventually worked was not more care at the
moment of fixing, it was a graded real-data benchmark that runs after every change. It held at 3 of
5 through all four resolver fixes this round.

**Two more resolver defects where my fix had removed something** (H2, H3). Merging two records
rebuilt the `Candidate` without `reading`, so it defaulted to the intact-query value and
corroboration *manufactured* an intact match: "Durham Coffee" was correctly ambiguous until an
Overture copy of a shop called "Coffee" arrived within 60 m. And `_split_locality` stripped the
boundary connective inside itself, so the ladder never held the rung "locality removed, connective
intact" — `Durham The Coffee` scored only `Coffee` at 1.000 while `The Coffee` got 0.725.

**A grid envelope that does not bound the grid** (M6). A UTM rectangle's top edge is a curve in
latitude with its maximum in the edge's interior, so four corners are not a bound; Codex's
counterexample put 806 cell centres outside one. The Triangle's envelope happened to agree with a
dense check, so the 522,160-building acquisition was never short — the *method* was wrong and would
have been quietly wrong for a differently placed metro. Now sampled along the boundary and padded
outward.

**A tie rule that was asymmetric** (M7). `cost_end < cost_start` won outright while the time
preference applied only inside an `np.isclose` band, so reversing an edge's stored geometry changed
the answer: 90 s versus 10 s on Codex's two-way segment. Now exact cost equality with ties broken by
time, which is order-invariant and is also what the contract actually says. Pass-11 M2's enumeration
of ordering dependencies was incomplete: **nearest-edge STRtree ties** and **equal-size component
selection** are added to S4.1.

**And a verification that could pass a difference that changes the answer** (L1). A 0.01 s tolerance
accepts stored float32 600.005 against rebuilt 599.999, two values on opposite sides of a
600-second threshold. float32 rounding is a known function, not an unknown error to absorb, so the
check now casts to float32 and demands exact equality — and it **passes exactly**: 0 cells differ,
maximum absolute difference 0.000000 s. The report also records `verification_bypassed` when
`--allow-artifact-mismatch` is used, so a bypassed run cannot be read as a verified one.

**One claim of mine was true but overreached.** "Nothing crosses a 10/15/25/45-minute threshold in
the losing direction" is four data points, not a property; Codex found cell (126, 612) failing at
**47 minutes**.

## S3 — Grid, encoding and what we may claim about accuracy

> **The executable design is predeclared in
> [`2026-11-04-s3-measurement-design.md`](./2026-11-04-s3-measurement-design.md)**, written before
> any sampling: estimands E1/E2/E3, four strata (boundary-adjacent and within-one-encoding-increment
> kept as separate populations), pilot and main allocations with the design-effect rule, weights,
> Wilson intervals with block bootstrap, and the missing-data treatment. The sections below are the
> requirements it satisfies; where the two differ, the design document is the operative one.


**S3.1 Candidate grids must be evaluated at matched locations.** Codex executed `TRIANGLE.grid()` at
both resolutions and they are **not nested**: 100 m origin 661900/4003500 (643 × 514), 200 m origin
661800/4003600 (322 × 258). Sampling each grid at its own cell centres would confound resolution with
alignment and location. Use **one set of residential coordinates and destination entrances
throughout**, and evaluate each candidate grid and encoding at those same points.

**S3.2 The decomposition, corrected.** Revision 1 claimed three separable components; encoding
appeared in two of them and "model error" bundled network, snapping, entrance and route-choice
differences together. Retain four **paired** values per sampled location and report signed paired
differences — never sums or medians of medians:

| value | what it is |
|---|---|
| exact route to the location | the engine's own answer at that coordinate |
| float surface at its cell centre | our reconstruction, pre-encoding |
| encoded surface | what the product actually ships |
| independent prediction | a different engine's opinion, matched arrival time |

The fourth is **engine disagreement**, not model error, until its causes are controlled.

**S3.3 The audit, with precision declared in advance.** Revision 1 chose 60 cells per side per
threshold with no estimand. Codex's arithmetic: with 60 independent observations and **zero**
observed failures the one-sided 95% upper bound on the rate is **4.87%**, not zero; roughly **385
observations per rate denominator** gives a five-point worst-case margin, and clustering raises it.
So predeclare: the estimand, the sampling frame, the band definition (spatial adjacency and one
encoding increment are different populations), strata and allocations, weights, confidence
intervals, and missing-data treatment. Treat 60 per side as a **pilot** whose output is a variance
estimate, not a verdict.

Boundary-only sampling is also insufficient on its own: a pipeline that wrongly deletes a
neighbourhood never samples that neighbourhood. Add an **independent residential sample** and a
**predicted-unreachable stratum**.

**S3.4 Three outcomes, not a pass/fail.** This is the honesty mechanism revision 1 got wrong by
labelling the engineering gate "decision 12":

1. **Construction fidelity: PASS / FAIL.** Our pipeline against exact routes from the same engine.
   Ours to fix; a failure is a defect.
2. **Engine disagreement: reported, with magnitude.** Descriptive.
3. **Real-world accuracy: supported / failed / UNVERIFIED.** Decision 12 stays **unverified** until
   independent observations support it. Revision 1's assumptions table required *both* Google
   disagreement *and* confirming owner journeys before weakening the claim, which made an untaken or
   inconclusive journey an escape hatch. One condition, stated plainly: absent independent
   observation, the claim is unverified and the label says so.

Shipping with an unverified accuracy claim is a legitimate product decision for a friends-and-family
release. It is the owner's to make explicitly, not something a measurement design should grant by
construction.

**S3.5 Then freeze the encoding.** Only now, with S0's statuses and S3.1's measurements in hand.
Carry forward from revision 1, corrected:

- **Legacy codes 251–254 are valid minute values today**, verified. Any sentinel scheme must
  **version the format** and rebuild or namespace existing artifacts; it may not reinterpret them.
- **The writer, exporter and browser decoder change together.** `grid.py:76` fixes dtype to uint8,
  `layer.py:61` uses that profile, and `g3-render/worker.js:144` recognises exactly one sentinel. A
  Python round-trip test cannot protect the browser; test actual browser decoding. Categorical
  status codes must never be interpolated during reprojection.
- **State bounds as bounds.** Whole-minute rounding contributes *up to* 30 s; that is a worst case at
  T = 10, not a routine half of the budget. Half-minutes still round; integer seconds are not exact
  for fractional inputs.
- Define the **supported maximum duration** first (it follows from S0.4's transit horizon and the
  relaxation limits), then choose a resolution that covers it. For reference, the current Triangle
  walk layer is **174 KB** deflated at uint8 whole minutes with 121 distinct values and a maximum of
  exactly 120 — the cutoff — so a cell *at* the horizon is today indistinguishable from a genuine
  120-minute answer.
- Measure compressed transfer size **and phone decode latency** per candidate, not just size.

## Assumptions, and what would invalidate them

| assumption | invalidated if | consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Construction fidelity is achievable to tolerance | S1.4 or S3.4(1) fails | the primitive is wrong; no label fixes it |
| A static model is adequate for a relocation decision | independent observations show otherwise | a departure-time-aware tier, or a weaker claim — **the claim is unverified until then regardless** |
| No-data regions are mostly unpopulated | S2.4 finds buildings in them | a network or access defect, ahead of all S5 work |
| One 8 GB VPS serves the on-demand tier | S5.5 misses its declared targets | 4 vCPU; the deferred budget decision returns |
| Browser-side algebra is fast enough on a phone | S5.4's phone run fails | server-rendered mask, already identified |
| Valhalla's bike profile is sound | untested — the spot check is **drive-only** | a bike calibration task returns; dropping our own unused bike fallback did not validate Valhalla's |
| A drive **category** layer is accurate enough to publish | **already measured**: a multi-destination surface is an upper bound on min-over-destinations time (+131 s at 3 POIs, +0 s at 1), and 5 of 12 reference-positive cells were missed at a 10-minute threshold on a conditional sample | S3 carries the selection term explicitly, or drive thresholds below ~15 minutes are not offered for category criteria; anchor criteria are unaffected |
| The expansion reconstruction itself is faithful | **supported**: single-destination drive matches the router at +0.0 s median | — but walk's node-level p90 of 30 s and maximum of 632 s remain unexplained |
| The access leg is a reasonable model | untested — `ACCESS_SPEED_FACTOR` is a prior and is now the larger bias term for walk | an access-leg calibration task, which affects every mode and both engines |

**Priors, labelled as such** (not measured here): one vertical slice before breadth is an
implementation prior, not an established ordering; the 450/120 s access penalties are Valhalla
defaults; hand-timed journeys are valuable independent observations but cannot certify a boundary
p90, and are not the only possible independent source.
